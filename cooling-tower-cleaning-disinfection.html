SVC 04
Cleaning

Chemistry can't shift what's already settled.

Even with a good treatment programme, towers collect sludge in the pond, scale on the fill and biofilm in the places water doesn't move much. That's why cooling systems need taking out of service for a proper clean. The 2024 edition of HSG274 Part 1 ties the frequency to how quickly your system fouls, judged by inspection and your treatment history. As a typical guide it gives at least every three months for a tower in a dirty environment, at least twice a year for air conditioning comfort cooling, and at least every 12 months for a clean industrial application.

We follow the sequence the guidance describes: disinfect the water before anyone opens the tower up, so the clean itself doesn't throw contaminated spray around; then physically clean; then disinfect again before the system goes back into normal service.

We plan the work with you, which matters a lot on sites where the cooling can't simply be switched off. Not sure how often your system really needs doing? Our article on how often a cooling tower should be cleaned covers it.

  • Pre-clean disinfection

    Online chlorination (or a suitable alternative) to cut the bacterial load before the tower is opened.

  • Drain down and physical clean

    Pond, sump, strainers, distribution, fill and drift eliminators cleaned and debris removed.

  • Inspection while it's open

    Condition of the fill, eliminators, casing, fan and pipework noted, with photos for your records.

  • Refill and post-clean disinfection

    The system is disinfected again, then flushed and brought back to normal treatment levels.

  • Resilient-site planning

    For sites that can't lose cooling, we plan around N+1 towers, cell-by-cell isolation or quieter periods.

  • Certificate and report

    A record of what was done, chemical levels, contact times and findings, ready for the log book.

Questions

Cleaning & disinfection: common questions

How often does a cooling tower need cleaning?

As often as inspection shows it's fouling. HSG274 Part 1 (2024) gives typical intervals of at least every three months for a tower in a dirty environment, at least twice a year for comfort cooling and at least every 12 months for a clean industrial system. Many sites still clean six-monthly as a precaution. Your risk assessment and written scheme set the actual frequency.

Can you clean one cell while the others keep running?

Often, yes. On multi-cell towers or sites with standby units, we can isolate and clean one at a time. We'll look at the layout and plan the safest way to do it.

Do evaporative condensers need cleaning too?

Yes. They need regular cleaning and disinfection as part of the written scheme. The frequency should come from your risk assessment, taking account of the guidance in HSG274 Part 1.

What happens if you find damage while the tower's open?

We'll record it with photos and tell you what we've found and how urgent it is. Repairs or replacement parts are quoted separately, so there are no surprises.

Does a tower need cleaning after a long shutdown?

Usually, yes. HSG274 Part 1 says a system should be inspected, disinfected and cleaned if needed after a shutdown of a month or longer, and sooner if the risk assessment says so, especially in summer. In healthcare premises that can be after as little as five days. The same applies before first use and after alterations such as new pumps or pipework.

How long does a clean take?

It depends on the size of the tower, how dirty it is and how easy it is to get at. A small packaged unit is a very different job from a large multi-cell tower. We'll give you a planned duration in the quote, including the disinfection contact times, so you can plan cooling cover around it.
