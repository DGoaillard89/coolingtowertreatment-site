Shutdown & start-up

Standing water is where the trouble starts.

A cooling tower that's sitting idle with water in it is a problem waiting to happen. With no circulation and no dosing, biocide levels fall away and bacteria get going. Then the day it's switched back on, it can throw the lot out as drift.

For short idle periods the usual answer is to keep water circulating with treatment running. For longer shutdowns, the system is normally drained, cleaned and left dry, then cleaned and disinfected again before it comes back into service. Your risk assessment and written scheme should spell out which applies, and we'll help you follow it.

New towers and refurbished systems need commissioning too. HSG274 Part 1 expects new systems to be cleaned and disinfected before they're put into use, with treatment and monitoring in place from day one. See our cleaning and disinfection page for how we go about it.

  • Planned shutdown

    Drain down or keep circulating, depending on how long it'll be idle and what your risk assessment says.

  • Start-up after idle periods

    Cleaning and disinfection where needed, refill, treatment established and checks before full load.

  • Commissioning of new plant

    Pre-commission clean and disinfection, dosing set up, baseline samples and records opened.

  • Seasonal systems

    Towers that only run in summer, planned around the start and end of the season.

  • Notification reminders

    New towers need notifying to the local authority; decommissioned ones need notifying too.

  • Records for the log book

    What was done, when, and the follow-up monitoring results.

Questions

Shutdown, start-up & commissioning: common questions

How long can a cooling tower sit idle before it's a problem?

There's no single safe number. Stagnant water quickly loses its biocide reserve, so even a few days without circulation can matter. Your written scheme should say what happens for short and long idle periods. If it doesn't, we'd recommend getting that added.

Do we need to disinfect before restarting?

After an extended shutdown, HSG274 Part 1 expects the system to be cleaned and disinfected before it goes back into service. For short breaks with treatment maintained, a check of chemical levels and a dip slide may be enough. We'll advise based on your system.

We're installing a new tower. When should you get involved?

As early as you can. We can advise on dosing points, sample points and access for cleaning before it's built, then commission it with a clean, disinfection and baseline sampling.

Does a new tower need notifying?

Yes. The Notification of Cooling Towers and Evaporative Condensers Regulations 1992 require you to notify the local authority in writing. You also need to tell them if a notified tower or condenser is taken out of use for good.

What if a tower is only off for a week or two?

Keep the treatment going. HSG274 Part 1 says that if a system will be out of use for a week or more, up to about a month, biocide should still be dosed and circulated through the whole system at least weekly, running the fans long enough to mix it properly. Beyond a month, drain it down and shut it properly, then recommission before reuse.

How is a new system commissioned?

It's cleaned and disinfected before it's put into use, treatment and monitoring start from day one, and HSG274 Part 1 says Legionella sampling should begin weekly until the system is shown to be under control. We set up dosing, bleed and sample points, take baseline readings and hand over a log book that's ready to use.
