The rules

Three documents your cooling tower lives by.

If you're the dutyholder for a site with wet cooling, these are the ones that matter. We work to all three, and we'll happily walk you through what they mean for your site.

HSE · L8

ACOP L8

The Approved Code of Practice for controlling Legionella in water systems. It sets out the dutyholder's responsibilities: assess the risk, have a written scheme, appoint a responsible person, monitor and keep records.

HSE · HSG274 Pt 1

HSG274 Part 1

The technical guidance for evaporative cooling systems. It covers treatment, monitoring frequencies, action levels for TVC and Legionella, and cleaning and disinfection at least twice a year.

SI 1992/2225

Notification Regulations 1992

The Notification of Cooling Towers and Evaporative Condensers Regulations 1992 require you to tell your local authority, in writing, about wet cooling towers and evaporative condensers on your premises.

What treatment controls

Four ways a cooling system goes wrong.

A good programme keeps all four in balance. They're connected, so fixing one while ignoring another rarely works for long.

  • 01Inhibitor + bleed

    Scale

    Hardness salts concentrate as water evaporates and coat the hottest surfaces, cutting efficiency and sheltering bacteria.

  • 02Inhibitor

    Corrosion

    Pipework, heat exchangers and casings slowly eaten away. Corrosion products also feed bacterial growth.

  • 03Dispersant + cleaning

    Fouling

    Dust, pollen and debris pulled in from the air settle in the pond and fill, soaking up biocide.

  • 04Biocides

    Microbiology

    General bacteria, biofilm and Legionella. Kept down with a biocide regime and checked by dip slides and lab samples.

FAQs

Quick answers

The questions we get asked most. There are more on our FAQs page

Which regulations apply to cooling towers?

The Health and Safety at Work etc. Act 1974 and COSHH set the general duties. ACOP L8 explains how to control Legionella, and HSG274 Part 1 gives the technical guidance for evaporative cooling systems. The Notification of Cooling Towers and Evaporative Condensers Regulations 1992 require you to notify the local authority of wet cooling towers and evaporative condensers on your site.

Do I need to notify the local authority about my cooling tower?

Yes, if you have a wet cooling tower or evaporative condenser, you need to notify the local authority in writing under the 1992 Notification Regulations, and tell them if a unit is taken out of use for good. Your council will have a form for it.

How often should a cooling tower be cleaned?

HSG274 Part 1 says cooling towers should be cleaned and disinfected at least twice a year. Your risk assessment may call for more frequent cleaning.

How often should we test for Legionella?

At least quarterly for cooling systems, plus more often in certain situations, such as at commissioning or after a positive result. Dip slides for general bacteria should be done at least weekly.
