The M4 corridor runs on cooling

Follow the M4 west out of London and you're soon in one of the busiest stretches of cooling plant in the country. Slough is one of the UK's best-known data centre clusters, with many halls on or near the Slough Trading Estate and new ones still being built. Reading, Bracknell and Maidenhead add business parks and corporate campuses running chilled-water systems, and the Heathrow side of the patch, around Colnbrook and Poyle, is full of warehousing and cold storage.

Not every site here has an open tower. Lots of the newer halls use dry coolers or adiabatic units, and those still need a Legionella risk assessment if they wet a surface or make spray. HSG274 Part 1 is clear that any unit able to produce aerosol needs its risk assessing. But open towers and evaporative condensers are still common on older halls, process sites and food distribution, and they're the ones that need a full treatment programme.

Working inside data centre change control

If you run a data hall you'll know the drill: method statements, change requests, approved windows, escorts and badge access. We work within it rather than around it. In practice that means:

  • cleaning one cell or one tower at a time while the N+1 capacity carries the load,
  • raising dosing changes and setpoint adjustments as changes, not quiet tweaks,
  • taking samples at agreed times with escorts booked ahead,
  • writing reports in a format your auditors and operations team already use.

Vetting and inductions take time on a first visit, so we build them into the start of the programme. Our article on data centre water treatment goes into more detail.

Water in the Thames Valley

Reading, Newbury and most of Maidenhead get their water from Thames Water. Slough is split, with Affinity Water serving parts of the town and its surroundings, and South East Water supplies parts of east Berkshire around Bracknell and Wokingham. Most of the area's water is hard, drawn from the Thames and the chalk. Your supplier's postcode checker will tell you which side of the line you're on.

For a large data centre tower, hardness has a real cost. On a hot day evaporation is heavy, and every litre that goes up the stack leaves its calcium behind. Set the bleed too tight and you waste water and chemical. Set it too loose and condenser tubes scale up on plant you can't easily take offline. Many larger sites here soften or otherwise pre-treat the make-up. If yours does, the softener needs watching too, because one missed regeneration sends hard water into a system tuned for soft.

Notification in Berkshire

Berkshire has no county council. It's six unitary councils: Slough, Reading, Wokingham, Bracknell Forest, Windsor and Maidenhead, and West Berkshire. Notification under the 1992 regulations goes to whichever one your site sits in, through your local council's environmental health team. On a campus with several towers, every one goes on the notice, and towers added during an expansion need notifying as well.

Getting there

From Chiswick we're on the M4 within minutes, which makes Slough and the Heathrow side easy to reach for routine visits, with Reading and Newbury further along the same road. Business parks usually have parking and ground-level plant yards. Data centres are the opposite: everything goes through the gatehouse, so we agree vehicle details and visit slots in advance.

Questions

Cooling towers in Thames Valley & Berkshire: common questions

Can you treat data centre cooling towers in Slough without risking uptime?

Yes. We plan cleans cell by cell or around N+1 capacity, raise dosing and setpoint changes through your change control, and book sampling visits and escorts in advance. Monitoring and records follow HSG274 Part 1, presented in a way your audit and operations teams can use.

Which council do we notify about a cooling tower in Slough or Reading?

The unitary council your site sits in, through its environmental health team. Berkshire has six: Slough, Reading, Wokingham, Bracknell Forest, Windsor and Maidenhead, and West Berkshire. The Notification of Cooling Towers and Evaporative Condensers Regulations 1992 require written notice of each wet tower and evaporative condenser.

Do adiabatic coolers on the M4 corridor need Legionella control?

If they spray water or wet a surface in the air stream, the risk needs assessing. HSG274 Part 1 asks you to check adiabatic and hybrid units against the definitions in the Notification Regulations, and to assess any unit that can make aerosol even if it isn't notifiable. We can help you work out where yours sit.

Is the water hard in Slough and Reading?

Mostly, yes. Thames Water supplies much of the area and Affinity Water serves parts of Slough, and both draw heavily on the Thames and chalk. That limits safe cycles of concentration, so bleed control and inhibitor choice matter. Check your postcode with your supplier and we'll test the make-up on site.
